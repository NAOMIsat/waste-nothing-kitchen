03 · The honest part

I made myself three promises while I wrote this.

"Every trick works!"

If a famous trick is a myth, I say so.

Even the ones I believed for years. Raw potatoes don't freeze, and I'll tell you why.

"Always make it yourself!"

If the cheap way is worse, I tell you to buy the thing.

Frozen peas from the store are often the better deal. I say so.

"Grandma never got sick!"

Where old habits and food safety disagree, safety wins.

Every storage time and safety note follows USDA guidance.

That's why every chapter ends the same way. This is the end of Chapter 42:

The short version

  • Wrap one bowl of rice in plastic wrap while it's still hot, pressed into a flat square.
  • Cool until just warm, then freeze flat. Use within about a month.
  • Microwave in the wrap until steaming hot.

The honest catch

  • Cooked rice shouldn't sit out for hours. Get it into the freezer within an hour or so.
  • It uses a lot of plastic wrap. Small reusable rice containers work too.

04 · Who's telling you this

A storeroom in the snow, and one rule.

Some winters, the snow where I grew up came up past the first-floor windows and the roads closed for days. So every autumn, my grandmother filled the house with food, and she wasted none of it. She said the same thing every day:

"If it still has value, it does not go in the trash."

My mother took that rule and gave it a freezer. My first year in America, I watched good, careful people throw away half a cabbage, a bag of spinach, a forgotten package of chicken, every week. Nobody had shown them another way. This book is everything I'd tell you across my kitchen table.

— Naomi
